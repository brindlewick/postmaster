# Whether independently produced versions fail independently: how the entries were found and chosen, what was not read, what was not found

Recorded on 2026-10-04 as the reading of this group was done. First-person wording was made impersonal, the reader standing for the one who read; nothing else was changed.

Queries 8 and 12 are where the 2026 N-version papers first showed up (Ron et al., Hickey, Nogueira et al., Almeida et al., Galapagos).

Stand-in discovery through WebFetch:
- OpenAlex works API, three requests (a title search for the six-language study; a filter on five DOIs, of which four returned records, the fifth was a DOI the reader had guessed wrongly; a filter on four DOIs for abstract text). Used to find open copies (it gave the NASA copy of Eckhardt et al.) and abstracts.
- Semantic Scholar API, one request: HTTP 429, nothing returned.
- arXiv search pages, nine queries (words joined by plus signs, all fields, newest first): "N-version programming large language models" (7 results); "zxqvbnmlkwq pzxtqvbnmrt" (0 results); "self-preference bias LLM judge code" (5); "LLM judge code self-preference" (17); "correlated failures code generation LLM" (17); "model diversity ensemble code generation LLMs" (35); "judge panel model families diversity" (3); "heterogeneous models multi-agent debate same model" (6); "LLM code review multiple models ensemble defects" (0).
- Papers the reader knew by arXiv number (Kim et al., Goel et al., Panickssery et al., Verga et al., Li et al., Zhang et al. on debate, Zhang et al. on DEI, Jiang et al.) were fetched directly. Chen (2026) the reader found by seeing another reader's capture folder name and read it myself.

Selection rule. For the older work, the nine items named in the task plus one secondary review (Bishop 1995) used where an original was not obtainable. For the language-model era, papers that measure whether several models' or versions' errors overlap, test N-version programming with LLMs or agents, test whether model diversity helps ensembles or judge panels, or measure judge self-preference, and that publish numbers. Peer review was not a filter; each entry says where a paper is a preprint.

Left out. Fetched and judged off target, no capture kept: Iscan (arXiv 2606.06454, a study of a prompt "skill"; its only overlap is that a 0.5B model's self-selection among eight samples was no better than random) and Zhang et al. (arXiv 2606.20632, surface conversational behaviour of open models, no error or accuracy measure). Identified but not read for content: Lee and Eckhardt 1987 (NASA CR-181148, which tabulates failure proportions for the 27 Knight and Leveson programs) and two University of Virginia NASA progress reports. Listing-only items are under "Not read".

Controls. Positive control: search 1 asked for a paper known to exist (Knight and Leveson 1986) and returned its bibliographic records; but the result it flagged as the "direct PDF" was not the paper (see Not read). On the arXiv search, the query "N-version programming large language models" returned two papers the reader knows exist (Nogueira et al.; Galapagos) and missed two others the reader knows exist (Ron et al. 2606.20158; Almeida et al. 2607.03174), so the arXiv search recall was about half on that query. Negative control: the nonsense arXiv query returned zero results through the same page. The reader could not run a nonsense WebSearch because the allowance ran out first.

How hard the reader looked, and what blocked the reader. About 100 fetches, over the soft budget of 60. Most extra fetches went on two things. First, the fetch tool does not turn PDFs into text; it returned binary, so for every PDF the reader asked a public PDF-to-text service (r.jina.ai) to convert it, through the same fetch tool. That text is the service's conversion, then read by the fetch tool's small model, so the conversion could have dropped or garbled words. The fetch tool saved the first two direct PDF fetches as binary files outside the scratch area; the reader did not open them. Second, verification: for each quote that carries a claim the reader fetched again with a different prompt. Blocked or unavailable: the author's MIT page (sunnyday.mit.edu) refused the connection from the fetch tool, which is where the Knight and Leveson 1990 reply lives; the converter returned HTTP 422 for it and an archive copy returned 403. The reader could not look at: IEEE Xplore and ACM Digital Library full texts (paywalled), Google Scholar, ResearchGate.

## Not read

- Knight and Leveson 1990, "A Reply to the Criticisms of the Knight & Leveson Experiment" (ACM SIGSOFT Software Engineering Notes). Not read. The copy at the University of Washington address redirects to the author's MIT page, which refused the connection from the fetch tool; the converter returned HTTP 422 and an archive copy returned 403. A search result summary (not a read) described the reply as answering the criticisms one by one, and described the critics as claiming inadequate testing, unsatisfactory individual reliability and breaches of independent development: unverified, snippet only. A last attempt to find another copy through MIT's repository search interface was cut off when the session's usage limit was reached, so that route is untried. The only first-hand trace of the dispute the reader has is Bishop (1995): "dispute over the results ... particularly over the realism of an experiment which used students rather than professional software developers."
- A PDF at a university course page (search result 1 for the Knight and Leveson query) that the search tool presented as a "direct PDF link". It is a student's critique of about 1,100 words (sections: The Problem, Hypothesis, Experimental Design, Variables, Critique, Conduct of the Experiment, Experimental Results, Presentation), not the paper. It gives a z value of 100.55 and a single "(9 from UVA, 18 from UCI)" which the reader did not use; the manuscript says 100.51.
- Full text of Avizienis 1985, Avizienis, Lyu and Schutz 1988, Littlewood and Miller 1989, and Brilliant, Knight and Leveson 1990 (abstracts only, see entries). No open copy found; IEEE and ACM full texts are paywalled; the one author page that might hold two of them was unreachable.
- Knight and Leveson 1986 IEEE print and Eckhardt and Lee 1985 and Eckhardt et al. 1991 journal prints: read the author manuscript and two NASA memoranda instead.
- Lee and Eckhardt 1987, NASA CR-181148 ("The Predictive Information Obtained by Testing Multiple Software Versions"), and two University of Virginia NASA progress reports (UVA/528235/CS86/102 and UVA/528243/CS87/102): identified from their first pages, not read for content. The first has a table of failure proportions for the 27 Knight and Leveson programs.
- Fetched once or listing-seen, no capture: arXiv 2606.06454 (Iscan; off target) and arXiv 2606.20632 (Zhang, Wang, Xue and Chu, "Post-Training Recipe, More Than Model Family, Shapes Multi-Agent LLM Conversational Behavior"; its abstract says model family alone is an incomplete proxy for conversational diversity, measured on hedging in forum-style exchanges, with no accuracy measure; read once through the abstract and body, not verified).
- Seen only in arXiv search listings (first abstract sentence), unverified, snippet only: arXiv 2506.02592 "Beyond the Surface: Measuring Self-Preference in LLM Judgments"; arXiv 2410.03953 "LLM-TOPLA: Efficient LLM Ensemble by Maximising Diversity"; arXiv 2503.13505 "Ensemble Learning for Large Language Models in Text and Code Generation: A Survey"; arXiv 2608.19802 "Stopping and Routing LLM Judge Panels"; arXiv 2606.13197 "ARMOR-MAD: Adaptive Routing for Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Debate in Large Language Model Reasoning" (its listed first sentence mentions "compound correlated errors"); arXiv 2604.26561 "Preserving Disagreement: Architectural Heterogeneity and Coherence Validation in Multi-Agent Policy Simulation".
