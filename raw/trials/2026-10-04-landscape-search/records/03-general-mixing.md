# General language-model evidence on mixing models: how the entries were found and chosen, what was not read, what was not found

Recorded on 2026-10-04 as the reading of this group was done. First-person wording was made impersonal, the reader standing for the one who read; nothing else was changed.

## How the entries were found and chosen

Starting point: the ticket's list of leads. The reader checked every lead the reader could name against the arXiv records by fetching the arXiv API record for each id (two fetches covering 27 ids, plus one that added the record for "Talk Isn't Always Cheap"). Every id the reader had in mind matched the title the reader expected; dates, versions and venue comments in the entries below come from those records. All the named leads were read, two of them only in part: the AB-MCTS paper's multi-LLM appendix (the fetched text stops before the results, so the multi-LLM figures come from the developer's blog post) and the 2025 debate position paper (abstract only, because its HTML body did not come back).

Searches (all WebSearch, mode "standard"; search 4 came back mostly off target and the reader did not get to retry it in mode "extended" because the shared search budget ran out):
1. `Hamidieh 2026 multi-model mixture of agents weak models pool aggregation arXiv`: 9 results. Not the paper the reader wanted (the 2026 paper cites a "Hamidieh et al. 2026"), but it returned the 2026 paper's own listing and led to the "Power and Limitations of Aggregation in Compound AI Systems" and MOSAIC titles (not read).
2. `"When Does Combining Language Models Help" routing voting mixture-of-agents 2026`: 10 results; found arXiv 2606.27288, which the reader then read in full.
3. `Hamidieh mixture-of-agents multi-model weak agents pool degrades aggregation arXiv 2026`: the tool ran four sub-queries, 9 results on the first; it returned the original Mixture-of-Agents paper and unrelated pages; no "Hamidieh" paper.
4. `does model diversity help LLM ensembles heterogeneous models versus same model repeated sampling equal compute 2026`: 9 results, mostly off target (merging of domain experts, a vendor blog, a snippet about a "Diverse Fingerprint Ensemble" paper). The summary the search tool wrote ("heterogeneous ensembles outperform homogeneous repeated sampling") is the tool's own and is not used as evidence.
5. `multi-agent debate versus majority voting self-consistency same compute heterogeneous models arXiv 2026`: 9 results; it led the reader to "Talk Isn't Always Cheap" and to the 2026 single-agent-versus-multi-agent paper.
6. A sixth query (the Diverse Fingerprint Ensemble paper by name) was refused: the session's shared search budget (200) was used up. The reader issued six in total. So the reader could not search further; arXiv API listings (below) were the reader's substitute for finding newer papers.

arXiv API (plain GET through WebFetch): (a) id lists, as above; (b) title search `Rethinking Mixture of Agents`: 1 entry (the Self-MoA paper); (c) title search for "mixture of agents" or "mixture-of-agents", newest first, 25 results from 2024-09 to 2026-09, of which only 2606.27288, 2605.29116 and 2502.00674 are about whether mixing helps; the rest are method or domain papers (clinical, circuit design, serving, routing) and were not read; (d) a nonsense query.

Controls. Positive: query 1 returned the known 2026 paper's listing and query 3 returned the original Mixture-of-Agents PDF; the arXiv title search for "Rethinking Mixture of Agents" returned exactly the paper that exists under that name. Negative: the arXiv API query `all:zzqxvkw9plmq` returned "no entries" (totalResults 0). The reader could not run a nonsense query through WebSearch because the search budget was gone.

Selection rule. Kept a source if it (a) reports numbers that compare mixed models, one model sampled several times, an aggregator that reads candidates, or selection; or (b) measures how independent different models' errors are, or whether a judge favours its own family. Citation chasing from the 2026 paper's reference list added Choi et al.; searches added Wynn et al. and Tran and Kiela; the debate position paper (Zhang et al.), ReConcile, the independence and judge-bias papers (Correlated Errors, Great Models Think Alike, Self-Preference), the aggregator paper (AggLM) and the agent-scaling paper were added from the reader's own knowledge and confirmed in the arXiv API; none of those was found by search. Left out: method-only Mixture-of-Agents variants and domain papers (titles only), surveys, model-merging and mixture-of-experts work, and software-engineering papers (another reader). Not read because the leads were snippets only: see Not read.

How hard the reader looked. 6 search calls (5 returned), 4 kinds of arXiv API call, about 85 WebFetch calls, against the ticket's budget of about 60. The reader went over because the fetch tool answers through a small model that sometimes paraphrases or contradicts itself, so each quote that carries a claim needed a second fetch with a different prompt, and the PDF route returned only compressed streams (two attempts) so every full text is an arXiv HTML page or an ar5iv render. Long HTML pages are cut off by the tool before their appendices or reference lists (this affected the 2026 Mixture-of-Agents paper and the AB-MCTS appendix). The reader did not use Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, OpenReview or GitHub for this group.

Conventions in the entries. "Checked" and "not checked" follow the ticket and are recorded per quote in each capture's passages.md (captures are under staging/papers/<slug>/ and staging/articles/<slug>/). Where the reader gives a number as "from one fetch", a second fetch did not repeat it. The venue line replaces "adoption" for papers.

## Not read

Unverified, snippet only (a search result or a listing gave a title or a sentence; the reader did not fetch the text):
- "Power and Limitations of Aggregation in Compound AI Systems" (arXiv 2602.21556): unverified, snippet only; a search summary said it studies aggregation in a principal-agent framework.
- The "Diverse Fingerprint Ensemble" paper (an EACL 2026 Findings paper, per a search snippet): unverified, snippet only; the search tool's summary said it builds ensembles by balancing diversity and competence. My follow-up search for it was refused (search budget used up).
- "Beyond Majority Voting: LLM Aggregation by Leveraging Higher-Order Information" (a page title in search results): unverified, snippet only.
- A claim in a search summary that, in debates among three heterogeneous agents on six benchmarks, "roughly one in four divergent cases had the minority holding the correct answer": unverified, snippet only; the summary named no source the reader could identify.
- Titles only, from search results or the arXiv listing: MOSAIC (2606.03014), "Mixture-of-Models: Unifying Heterogeneous Agents via N-Way Self-Evaluating Deliberation" (2601.16863), "This Is Your Doge, If It Please You: Exploring Deception and Robustness in Mixture of LLMs" (2503.05856), DynaDebate (2601.05746), and the Mixture-of-Agents method and domain papers in the arXiv listing (for example 2609.25959, 2606.24437, 2606.03867, 2605.15706, 2601.18130, 2601.16596, 2505.24442, 2411.03284, 2503.21720). A heterogeneous domain-expert ensemble paper and a vendor blog about it came up in search 4 and are about merging experts, not mixing model outputs.
- The ICLR 2025 venue of the original Mixture-of-Agents paper: unverified, snippet only (a search summary and a text-mirror path); the arXiv record lists no venue.

Found as arXiv records but not read by the reader, and worth reading by anyone extending this: "Recursive Self-Aggregation Unlocks Deep Thinking in Large Language Models" (2509.26626, v2 24 Feb 2026), "Large Language Monkeys" (2407.21787) and "More Agents Is All You Need" (2402.05120, TMLR). They bear on aggregation by a model and on same-model sampling. Another reader has captures of the last two (staging/papers/brown-2024-largemonkeys and li-2024-moreagents) and of two papers that look related to this group's questions, "Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes: Correlated Errors Undermine LLM Evaluation Panels" (staging/papers/kohli-2026-nine-judges) and "Artificial Hivemind: The Open-Ended Homogeneity of Language Models (and Beyond)" (staging/papers/jiang-2025-hivemind); the reader has not read those captures or the pages and make no claim about them.

Not found: the cited "Hamidieh et al. 2026" (the 2026 Mixture-of-Agents paper's reference list gave, in one fetch, the title "Complementing self-consistency with cross-model disagreement for uncertainty quantification" at ICLR; the entry was not re-checked and the reader could not find the paper).
